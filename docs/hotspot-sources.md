# National hotspot sources and responsible access

Reviewed **23 October 2026**. This is a representative selection of public birding
places across all **17 New Zealand regions**, including the Chatham Islands. It
is **not exhaustive**, a live sightings service, a population survey, or a promise
that every target will be visible on a visit. An established sanctuary population
can be a worthwhile target without being common or easy to see.

## Scope and evidence standard

- All 33 pre-existing national hotspot IDs, coordinates and target memberships
  are retained. No existing target was removed or substituted.
- Eight existing places gain 36 species–place associations. Two new places add
  six more: **42 target associations added**, including **19 distinct species
  from the expanded catalogue** that previously had no national hotspot targets.
- There are now **35 national additions**, alongside the unchanged 25 legacy
  Canterbury places: **60 hotspots overall**. Auckland remains exactly three
  places; the Chatham Islands remain exactly one.
- Additions below require a current site-specific description from DOC, the
  managing trust/council, or the official local visitor organisation. Species
  ranges, eBird sightings, search snippets, old release announcements alone, and
  neighbouring sites are not sufficient evidence of a reliable target here.
- “Current” means the public pages consulted on the review date; some pages are
  undated and their population information may lag field conditions. Check the
  linked manager's alerts and visitor information before travelling.
- Regional access references below support the stated place/access facts, not
  an independent re-verification of every retained legacy target. An unchanged
  target list must not be read as an exhaustive official checklist.
- `HOTSPOTS.targets` remains the source for the species-detail place list. No
  species reverse arrays, catalogue taxonomy, saved IDs or scoring are changed.

## Documented additions at existing places

### Tiritiri Matangi — `tiritiri-matangi` (Auckland)

**Added:** `hihi`, `takahe`, `north-island-kokako`,
`north-island-saddleback`, `whitehead`, `north-island-robin`,
`red-crowned-parakeet`, `titipounamu`.

The Supporters of Tiritiri Matangi's [current wildlife descriptions][tiri-birds]
describe these established island populations, including hihi, kōkako, tīeke,
pōpokotea, takahē, toutouwai, kākāriki and rifleman. Their descriptions identify
the kākāriki as red-crowned and the robin as North Island robin. The site's
North Island tīeke and kōkako correspond to the separate North Island catalogue
IDs; `takahe` is the living South Island takahē species, including translocated
birds on North Island sanctuaries.

Kōkako are explicitly described as elusive, not guaranteed. No feeder, nest-box,
territory or nest coordinates are included. The existing wharf pin stays put.
[Trip planning][tiri-visit] covers ferry travel, guided walks and biosecurity;
overnight accommodation needs a separate booking. Do not play calls or feed birds.

**Deliberate exclusions:** the [sanctuary account][tiri-sanctuary] separates
visiting, non-breeding kākā from residents. The wildlife page describes problems
with pāteke persistence, so neither is added as a reliable target here. Little
spotted kiwi are not added to this predominantly daytime ferry destination.

### Rotorua Sulphur Point — `rotorua-sulphur-point` (Bay of Plenty)

**Added:** `new-zealand-dabchick`.

[RotoruaNZ's Sulphur Bay Wildlife Refuge description][sulphur] explicitly names
New Zealand dabchick at the bay beside Government Gardens. It also states that
birds breed here, that visitors must stay on designated paths, and that dogs are
not permitted. The addition applies to viewing the bay from the existing public
shore access, not entering nesting habitat or walking on geothermal ground.
The retained red-billed gull target is not silently replaced with black-billed
gull: the source naming the latter does not establish that the former is wrong.

### Pekapeka Wetland — `pekapeka` (Hawke's Bay)

**Added:** `new-zealand-dabchick`, `little-black-shag`.

[Hawke's Bay Regional Council][pekapeka] specifically lists both among birds
visitors might see. Little black shag is a separate species from the retained
little shag (`kawaupaka`). The boardwalks and lookout provide the public viewing
route; reeds and nesting areas do not. The council lists bittern as very rare,
so its appearance on the wider species list does not justify adding it as a
reliable target. No dogs; the first weekend of May shooting closure is retained.

### Rotokare — `rotokare` (Taranaki)

**Added:** `hihi`, `north-island-saddleback`, `north-island-robin`,
`whitehead`, `brown-teal`, `titipounamu`.

The trust's [bird page][rotokare-birds] names all six. Its [sanctuary account][rotokare-sanctuary]
also describes restored, successfully breeding populations, rather than merely
historic releases. [Tracks and facilities][rotokare-visit] describe the lake
walkway and boardwalk; the [trust's visitor introduction][rotokare-home] confirms
free public entry and the no-pets rule. The existing entrance pin is unchanged.
No kiwi target is added to an otherwise daytime walk, despite the documented
North Island brown kiwi population. Do not seek roosts or use playback.

### Zealandia — `zealandia` (Wellington)

**Added:** `kaka`, `little-spotted-kiwi`, `hihi`, `takahe`, `whitehead`,
`north-island-saddleback`, `north-island-robin`, `red-crowned-parakeet`,
`brown-teal`, `titipounamu`.

Zealandia's [current bird guide][zealandia-birds] lists these species, explicitly
identifying the North Island robin/saddleback and red-crowned parakeet. The
[little spotted kiwi account][zealandia-kiwi] describes the resident population
and guided viewing, not a generic Wellington distribution.

**The kiwi target is represented here only as a booked guided night visit.**
The [Zealandia by Night page][zealandia-night] says sightings are not guaranteed,
even though kiwi are seen on most tours. It does not justify a daytime kiwi
promise or independent after-hours entry. Use the [tickets and tours page][zealandia-visit]
for current availability and conditions. The pin remains the public visitor
centre. Follow guides' lighting directions; no flash on nocturnal wildlife,
playback, feeding, nest searches or off-track following of calls.

### Lake Rotoiti, Kerr Bay — `lake-rotoiti` (Tasman)

**Added:** `south-island-robin`, `kaka`. **Retained:** `titipounamu` (rifleman)
was already a target and is not counted as an addition here.

DOC's [Kerr Bay walks description][rotoiti-walks], under the Honeydew Walk,
specifically mentions robins and possible kākā sightings from the walk reached
via Bellbird Walk. Its [Nelson Lakes account][nelson-lakes] also describes robin,
rifleman and kākā in the forest. Kākā may be heard rather than seen. The robin
maps to the South Island species, not the North Island robin.

The unchanged pin is at Kerr Bay. Use open Bellbird/Honeydew tracks, check alerts,
and do not feed wildlife. Park-wide references to alpine birds, kiwi or whio are
not projected onto this short, lakefront visit. Despite the “Nelson Lakes” name,
this hotspot is in **Tasman**, not Nelson city.

### Truman Track — `truman-track` (West Coast)

**Added:** `south-island-robin`.

DOC's [Truman Track page][truman], under **Nature and conservation → Birds**,
specifically includes New Zealand robin/toutouwai in the coastal forest on this
track. Geography resolves that name to the South Island robin catalogue entry.
No weka or kiwi is inferred from the wider Punakaiki area. Retain the public
car-park pin, highway-crossing warning and closed beach-step/cliff precautions.

### Ulva Island — `ulva-island` (Southland)

**Added:** `south-island-robin`, `weka`, `kaka`, `yellowhead`,
`south-island-saddleback`, `titipounamu`.

DOC's [current island description][ulva-doc], under **Nature and conservation →
Birds**, explicitly lists all these birds. The [official Stewart Island visitor
association][ulva-association] additionally specifies **South Island saddleback**,
yellowhead, rifleman and Stewart Island robin. In this catalogue Stewart Island
robin is included within `south-island-robin`, not a separate species ID.

The existing Post Office Bay landing pin is unchanged. DOC provides current
alerts and prohibits playback, feeding and pets. The [Ulva Island Charitable
Trust's visitor guidance][ulva-visit] specifies daylight visits, departure by
6 pm, no overnight stays, return water-taxi arrangements and at least 10 m space
for sea lions. Check bags/footwear and respect private land. DOC describes daytime
kiwi sightings as lucky/rare: `tokoeka` is **not** made a routine daytime target.

## Two new public places

### Arthur's Pass Village — `arthurs-pass-village` (Canterbury)

**Targets:** `kea` only. Approximate public visitor-centre-area pin:
**−42.9412, 171.5628**.

DOC's [kea page][kea] specifically names **Arthur's Pass village**, not just the
national park, as a place to see kea. Its [visitor-centre page][arthurs-visit]
provides public arrival/access information and currently directs visitors to a
temporary portacom opposite the old building during renovation. The pin is
cross-checked against the [mapped DOC visitor centre][arthurs-map]; follow current
signs rather than treating it as a building-door waypoint.

Use designated off-highway parking and public paths. Never feed, lure or handle
kea; secure food/gear and check around vehicles before moving. Do not follow a
bird onto SH73 or railway land. No alpine route, nest or kiwi-search area is
implied. Kea move around and are not guaranteed at the pin.

This supplies the kea gap without forcing an unsafe Milford Road stop. DOC's
[The Chasm page][chasm] displayed a construction-closure alert at review; no new
Chasm or Homer Tunnel roadside pin is added.

### Pukekura / Taiaroa Head — `taiaroa-head` (Otago)

**Targets:** `northern-royal-albatross`, `otago-shag`, `parekareka`,
`royal-spoonbill`, `tarapunga`. Approximate public visitor-centre pin:
**−45.7761, 170.7285**.

DOC's [Taiaroa Head visitor description][taiaroa-doc] distinguishes flying
**northern** royal albatross from colony viewing and explicitly identifies Otago
shag, spotted shag, royal spoonbill and red-billed gull. These are not southern
royal albatross or the separate Foveaux shag species. The red-billed gull's
unambiguous English name is mapped to `tarapunga`; the source's Māori label is
not used to accidentally select the catalogue's black-billed gull `tarapuka`.

The [Royal Albatross Centre FAQ][taiaroa-faq] confirms a free public viewing
platform and **no public access beyond the centre into the reserve**. Colony
views require a paid guided observatory tour; DOC says the observatory also gives
the best Otago shag views. Flights depend on weather and birds' movements;
October can have fewer/no visible albatross. No guaranteed free colony view is
implied. [Book through the operator][taiaroa-visit].

The [mapped centre building][taiaroa-map] and operator's address locate the pin
away from nests. Arrive via Harington Point Road on the Otago Peninsula, not
SH88 on the other side of the harbour. Stay on the public boardwalk, behind
barriers; no independent lighthouse access, pets, feeding or drones. Separate
evening penguin tours are not bundled into this site's targets.

## Exclusions, clarifications and ethical rules

- **Urban introduced birds:** `common-myna`, `spotted-dove` and
  `eastern-rosella` were considered for northern urban stops but not added in
  this review. No sufficiently specific current official description was
  verified for those species at the existing pins. Generic northern ranges and
  individual sightings are not substitutes. This is an evidence limit, **not** a
  claim that they are absent. Add later only with place-specific support.
- **Whio:** no `blue-duck` location is forced into this update. Park-wide river
  habitat references do not establish a safe, reliable public viewing point.
- **No new vagrant targets:** a bird appearing on a cumulative local checklist
  does not make it a dependable visitor target. Seasonal timing still matters
  for retained migrant targets.
- **No existing target corrections/removals.** Clarifications concern the
  correct island robin/saddleback IDs, little black versus little shag, northern
  royal albatross, guided/night access and non-guaranteed sightings. All original
  national coordinates and target memberships are preserved.
- Pins mark public entrances, arrival areas, landing places or viewpoints, never
  nest/roost coordinates. A species' association with a sanctuary is not a licence
  to find its nest, leave tracks, enter research areas or copy sensitive reports.
- No playback, chasing, baiting, handling, feeding or vegetation disturbance to
  obtain a tick or photograph. Observe managed feeding areas only as permitted;
  they do not authorise visitors to feed wildlife. Obey barriers and allow birds
  to move away. Leave dogs/pets behind where prohibited and follow biosecurity
  checks at islands and fenced sanctuaries.
- Tides, weather, road/track closures, ticket availability, temporary visitor
  facilities and species management can change. The manager's current rules take
  precedence. Neither an existing pin nor a historical sighting grants access to
  private land, offshore reserves, breeding colonies or dangerous terrain.
